## Body (paste as HTML)

<p>If your flavor personality is “bright and breezy,” this is your signature duo. Sauvignon Blanc’s citrus-and-herb snap meets fresh chèvre’s clean tang — like a picnic that packed itself.</p>

<p>It’s the pairing we reach for when the weather’s warm, the playlist is easy, and nobody wants anything heavy.</p>

<h2>Why this pairing works</h2>
<p>Fresh chèvre is lemony and light. Sauvignon Blanc (think Loire, New Zealand, or crisp California) brings grapefruit, grass, and mouthwatering acidity. Matching intensity + matching freshness = a combo that feels effortless and tastes intentional.</p>

<div>
  <h2>Pairing callout</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Wine:</strong> Sauvignon Blanc (crisp and dry — avoid heavily oaked styles for this match)</li>
    <li><strong>Cheese:</strong> Fresh chèvre (young goat cheese logs or tubs; soft and tangy)</li>
    <li><strong>Why it works:</strong> Acid loves acid — in a good way. Wine and cheese mirror each other’s brightness, so every sip and bite stays lively.</li>
    <li><strong>Serving tips:</strong> Chill the Sauvignon Blanc well. Keep chèvre cool. Serve with cucumber, cherry tomatoes, herbs, olive oil, or honey. Crusty bread optional; a fork-and-glass setup works too.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<h2>Serve it your way</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Picnic mode:</strong> pre-slice baguette, pack the chèvre, freeze a cold pack next to the bottle.</li>
  <li><strong>Herby glow-up:</strong> top chèvre with lemon zest, cracked pepper, and thyme.</li>
  <li><strong>Honey drizzle:</strong> a little sweet against the tang makes people go “ohhh.”</li>
</ul>

<h2>Bestie tip</h2>
<p>This is a fantastic “starter pairing” for friends who say they don’t like goat cheese. The wine’s citrus makes the chèvre taste friendlier — promise.</p>

<p><strong>Crisp, tangy, totally us.</strong> 🥂🧀</p>
