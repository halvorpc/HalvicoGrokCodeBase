## Body (paste as HTML)

<p>Pinot Noir and Brie are soft-spoken besties: lighter red fruit, earthy charm, and a cheese that melts into pure comfort. No heavy tannins to fight through — just silk meeting silk.</p>

<p>Perfect for date night, book club, or “we’re staying in and that’s the plan.”</p>

<h2>Why this pairing works</h2>
<p>Brie’s buttery rind-ripened richness loves Pinot’s bright red berries, gentle acidity, and soft tannins. The wine refreshes between bites; the cheese makes the Pinot taste plush. It’s balance without drama.</p>

<div>
  <h2>Pairing callout</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Wine:</strong> Pinot Noir (Burgundy-style, Oregon, California coast — look for elegant, not overly oaky)</li>
    <li><strong>Cheese:</strong> Brie (ripe and gooey at the center is ideal)</li>
    <li><strong>Why it works:</strong> Soft tannins + creamy fat. Pinot’s acidity cuts Brie’s richness; Brie amplifies the wine’s fruit and earth.</li>
    <li><strong>Serving tips:</strong> Let Brie sit out ~1 hour so it’s spreadable. Serve Pinot lightly chilled (cooler than a big Cab). Baguette, grapes, and a touch of berry jam optional but adorable.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<h2>Board ideas</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Whole small Brie as the centerpiece — score the top, bake briefly if you want extra melty drama.</li>
  <li>Keep accompaniments light: fruit, mild crackers, maybe prosciutto.</li>
  <li>One nice bottle of Pinot beats three “meh” ones. Quality over quantity tonight.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Bestie tip</h2>
<p>If your Brie still feels firm, give it more time on the counter. Cold Brie hides half its personality — same way fridge-cold Pinot can taste muted.</p>

<p><strong>Soft, cozy, completely bestie.</strong> 🍷🧀</p>
