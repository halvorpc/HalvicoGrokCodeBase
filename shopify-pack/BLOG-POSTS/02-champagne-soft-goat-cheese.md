## Body (paste as HTML)

<p>Champagne isn’t only for New Year’s — and soft goat cheese isn’t only for salads. Together they’re light, lively, and a little luxurious: the kind of pairing that makes a regular evening feel like a celebration.</p>

<p>Think citrusy bubbles meeting creamy tang. Instant bestie energy.</p>

<h2>Why this pairing works</h2>
<p>Soft goat cheese (chèvre-style logs or rounds) is bright and lactic. Champagne (or a dry traditional-method sparkler) brings acidity, fine bubbles, and toasty notes. The fizz scrubs the palate; the cheese softens the wine’s zip. Fresh, clean, totally snackable.</p>

<div>
  <h2>Pairing callout</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Wine:</strong> Champagne (Brut) — or a quality dry sparkling wine if you’re keeping it casual</li>
    <li><strong>Cheese:</strong> Soft goat cheese (fresh or lightly aged; creamy, spreadable)</li>
    <li><strong>Why it works:</strong> High acidity + creamy tang = lift. Bubbles cut richness; goat cheese loves citrus and toast notes in the wine.</li>
    <li><strong>Serving tips:</strong> Chill the bottle well. Serve goat cheese cool but not fridge-cold. Spread on crostini with honey, herbs, or thin cucumber slices. Flutes or white-wine glasses both work.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<h2>Easy ways to serve</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Brunch board:</strong> goat cheese, berries, baguette, a little jam.</li>
  <li><strong>Aperitif hour:</strong> just cheese, crackers, and bubbles — done.</li>
  <li><strong>Herb glow-up:</strong> roll the log in cracked pepper or chives before slicing.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Bestie tip</h2>
<p>No Champagne on hand? A dry Cava or Crémant keeps the same vibe. The rule is bubbles + brightness, not the price tag.</p>

<p><strong>Pop, spread, cheers.</strong> ✨🧀</p>
