## Body (paste as HTML)

<p>If Cab + cheddar is the reliable bestie, Port + blue is the one who shows up in velvet. Intensely sweet wine, intensely salty cheese — they shouldn’t work, and yet they absolutely do.</p>

<p>This is your after-dinner flex: small pours, big flavor, zero fuss.</p>

<h2>Why this pairing works</h2>
<p>Blue cheese is salty, funky, and creamy. Port is sweet, fortified, and full of dried fruit and spice. Salt amplifies sweetness; sweetness tames the blue’s punch. It’s contrast that clicks — like sweet-and-salty snacks, but elevated.</p>

<div>
  <h2>Pairing callout</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Wine:</strong> Port (Ruby for fruit-forward fun; Tawny for nutty, caramel vibes)</li>
    <li><strong>Cheese:</strong> Blue cheese (Stilton, Gorgonzola Dolce for milder, Roquefort for bold)</li>
    <li><strong>Why it works:</strong> Sweet vs. salty magic. Port’s residual sugar balances blue’s intensity; the cheese makes the wine taste even richer.</li>
    <li><strong>Serving tips:</strong> Small glasses, small wedges. Serve Port slightly cool. Add walnuts, pears, or dark chocolate on the side. A little goes a long way — that’s the charm.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<h2>Keep it simple</h2>
<ul>
  <li>One blue, one Port, one crunchy element (walnuts or water crackers).</li>
  <li>Offer a milder blue if guests are new to the style — Gorgonzola Dolce is a friendly on-ramp.</li>
  <li>Save this for the end of the night when people are ready to linger.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Bestie tip</h2>
<p>Tell the table: “Sweet wine, salty cheese — trust the plot twist.” Watching skeptics convert is half the fun.</p>

<p><strong>Bold besties forever.</strong> 🍷💙</p>
